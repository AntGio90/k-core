Item Definition: Multifunctional On-Board Charger (OBC) for BEV
Document ID: OBC-ITEM-DEF-001
Version: 1.0
Date: 30/09/2025

1. Item Name and Description
The item under consideration is the On-Board Charger (OBC), a power electronics system integrated into a Battery Electric Vehicle (BEV).

Its primary function is to manage the energy conversion between the external electrical grid and the vehicle's high-voltage (HV) battery. The item is designed to be multifunctional, including not only standard charging capabilities but also advanced features for power conversion and interaction with the grid and other vehicles.

2. Main Functionalities
The OBC is responsible for the following key functions:

AC/DC Charging (AC Charging): Converts single-phase or three-phase alternating current (AC) from an external source (e.g., home outlet, public AC charging station) into direct current (DC) to charge the vehicle's HV battery. This function must comply with international standards such as IEC 61851 and SAE J1772.

DC/DC Converter (LV Battery Charging): Integrates a DC/DC converter to charge and maintain the vehicle's low-voltage (LV, typically 12V) battery by drawing power from the high-voltage (HV) battery.

Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G): Implements a bidirectional power flow. Upon request, the OBC can convert the direct current (DC) stored in the HV battery back into alternating current (AC) and feed it into the electrical grid in a synchronized manner. This function must operate in compliance with grid interconnection standards (e.g., IEEE 1547, EN 50549) and communication protocols (e.g., ISO 15118).

Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) / Vehicle-to-Load (V2L): Allows the energy from the HV battery to be used to power external AC loads (V2L function) or to charge another electric vehicle (V2V function). The OBC acts as an AC voltage source through the vehicle's charging inlet.

3. Boundaries and Interfaces
The OBC is a distinct unit with the following main interfaces:

Power Interfaces:

AC Input: Connection to the vehicle's charging inlet to receive power from the electrical grid.

HV DC Output/Input: Connection to the vehicle's high-voltage bus to charge/discharge the HV battery.

LV DC Output: Connection to the vehicle's low-voltage bus to supply the 12V system.

Communication Interfaces:

CAN/CAN-FD Bus: Primary interface for communication with other ECUs in the vehicle, such as the Vehicle Control Unit (VCU) and the Battery Management System (BMS), to receive commands and send status data (e.g., current, voltage, temperature).

PLC (Power Line Communication): Communication with the charging infrastructure (EVSE) over the charging cable (Control Pilot), according to the ISO 15118 standard for managing smart charging and V2G functions.

Physical and Thermal Interfaces:

Chassis: Mechanical mounting points to the vehicle frame.

Cooling System: Inlet/outlet connections for coolant managed by the vehicle's thermal management system.

4. Operational Scenarios and Use Cases
The OBC operates in the following main scenarios:

AC Charging: The vehicle is connected to an AC charging station. The OBC manages the charging process based on grid limits and BMS requests.

Driving/Standby: The OBC is inactive for AC charging, but its DC/DC function is active to maintain the 12V battery.

V2G Mode: The vehicle is connected to a bidirectional charging station. Upon command (from the grid or user), the OBC reverses the power flow, supplying energy to the grid.

V2V/V2L Mode: The user activates the function via the vehicle's HMI. The OBC generates a stable AC voltage at the charging inlet to power an external device or another vehicle.

Diagnostics and Updates: States where diagnostic checks or software updates are performed.

5. Dependencies on Other Vehicle Items
The correct functioning of the OBC is critically dependent on the following items external to it:

Battery Management System (BMS): Provides critical battery parameters (State of Charge - SoC, State of Health - SoH, voltage and current limits) and authorizes the start and end of charging/discharging.

Vehicle Control Unit (VCU): The main control unit that interprets user and infrastructure requests, sending operational commands to the OBC.

Thermal Management System: Must provide active cooling to the OBC to keep it within its operational temperature limits.

Gateway/Communication Control Unit: Manages the routing of messages on the vehicle's communication buses.

6. Constraints
Regulatory: Must comply with all electrical safety regulations, electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) standards, and specific regulations for grid connection (Grid Codes).

Environmental: Must operate within a defined temperature and humidity range for automotive applications (e.g., -40°C to +85°C). Must have an appropriate IP protection rating (e.g., IP67).

Performance: Maximum charging/discharging power (e.g., 11 kW / 22 kW), conversion efficiency (e.g., > 94%), output power quality (THD).

7. Assumptions
It is assumed that the BMS is a separate item and is responsible for the monitoring and protection of the HV battery at the cell level.

It is assumed that the AC grid to which the item connects is stable and compliant with nominal specifications.

It is assumed that information received via communication buses (CAN, PLC) from other ECUs is correct and reliable.

8. Relevant Safety Aspects (Known Unsafe Behaviors)
A preliminary assessment identifies the following potential hazards, which will be analyzed in detail during the Hazard Analysis and Risk Assessment (HARA) phase:

Risk of electric shock: Hazardous voltage present on accessible interfaces under fault conditions.

Risk of fire: Overheating of internal components or overloading of the HV battery.

Damage to the HV battery: Over-charging or under-discharging of the battery due to an OBC malfunction.

Unsafe energy feed-in to the grid (V2G): Failure to synchronize or disconnect during a grid blackout (anti-islanding failure).

Unintended activation of V2G/V2V mode: Delivering power when not requested, leading to safety risks.
